# Starter Freshwater Content Plan

This document defines the recommended first freshwater roster, starter tank
archetypes, compatibility assumptions, and early career progression ladder.

It is intended to turn the higher-level game design into a concrete first-playable
content target.

## Design goals

- Make the first playable version legible and attractive
- Support distinct beginner fantasies immediately
- Teach players that tank design, compatibility, and maintenance matter
- Include at least one showcase fish, one breeding staple, one schooling species,
  and one cleanup role
- Keep the early catalog small enough to tune carefully

## Starter freshwater roster

The recommended first roster is intentionally small and role-driven.

### Tier 1 launch species

1. **Betta splendens (Betta)**
   - Role: solo display fish / centerpiece
   - Best use: small display tank
   - Teaches: territoriality, low-flow habitats, shelter, surface behavior

2. **Poecilia reticulata (Guppy)**
   - Role: early breeder and population-growth species
   - Best use: small breeder or community tank
   - Teaches: livebearing, rapid reproduction, strain building

3. **Paracheirodon innesi (Neon tetra)**
   - Role: peaceful schooling community fish
   - Best use: planted tropical community tank
   - Teaches: group size, schooling behavior, stress from poor tankmates

4. **Corydoras panda (Panda corydoras)**
   - Role: bottom-dwelling cleanup support fish
   - Best use: peaceful tropical community tank
   - Teaches: bottom-zone occupancy, social grouping, detritus cleanup support

### Tier 2 early unlock species

5. **Otocinclus affinis (Otocinclus)**
   - Role: algae-control support fish
   - Best use: stable planted community tank
   - Teaches: algae management, niche fish roles, tank maturity expectations

## Why this roster works

This first set gives players four distinct fantasies immediately:

- **Showpiece tank**: Betta
- **Breeding/profit tank**: Guppy
- **Schooling community tank**: Neon tetra
- **Maintenance support tank**: Panda corydoras and Otocinclus

It also teaches that "fish value" is not only about sale price. Some fish create
value by:

- making a tank look better
- stabilizing its ecosystem
- controlling algae or leftover waste
- enabling profitable breeding loops

## Starter tank archetypes

The first tank options should feel materially different, not just larger or smaller
boxes.

### 1. Betta display tank

- Size target: 5-gallon display
- Water profile: freshwater tropical, low flow
- Stocking expectation: one betta, no required tankmates
- Visual identity: planted, calm, decorative, backdrop-forward
- Core decor:
  - substrate
  - rooted plants
  - floating log or leaf rest
  - gentle heater/filter
  - custom or preset backdrop

Why it matters:

- supports the "single showcase fish" fantasy
- creates an accessible starting tank for artistic players
- demonstrates that not every tank is a community tank

### 2. Starter tropical community tank

- Size target: 10-gallon or 15-gallon
- Water profile: freshwater tropical
- Stocking expectation:
  - neon tetra school
  - panda corydoras group
  - optional future centerpiece once the tank is stable
- Visual identity: plants, open swim lane, rear backdrop, bottom shelter
- Core decor:
  - heater
  - filter
  - substrate
  - background image
  - a few low-risk plants and rocks/wood

Why it matters:

- teaches compatibility and layering of top/mid/bottom tank zones
- lets players see fish behave differently by role
- creates a strong observer-mode showcase candidate

### 3. Guppy breeder tank

- Size target: 10-gallon utility tank
- Water profile: freshwater tropical
- Stocking expectation:
  - breeding guppies
  - limited decorative spend
  - fry-protection layout or breeder accessories later
- Visual identity: more functional than artistic
- Core decor:
  - heater
  - filtration
  - plants or cover for fry
  - low-cost backdrop or bare-bones display

Why it matters:

- supports the breeder/tycoon fantasy early
- gives players a reason to choose profit over polish
- teaches population growth and resource tradeoffs

### 4. Cleanup-assisted planted tank

- Size target: 15-gallon tropical planted tank
- Water profile: freshwater tropical
- Stocking expectation:
  - neon tetra school
  - panda corydoras group
  - otocinclus once unlocked
- Visual identity: healthy planted tank with visible ecosystem balance
- Core decor:
  - richer plant coverage
  - stable substrate
  - wood/rock structure
  - algae-prone surfaces

Why it matters:

- teaches that algae and waste are gameplay systems
- demonstrates that cleanup species are support tools, not cosmetic filler
- encourages balance instead of one-dimensional profit play

## Compatibility matrix

Status key:

- **Good**: recommended pairing for standard play
- **Situational**: possible with tank size, cover, and careful setup
- **Avoid**: should be discouraged by warnings and behavior penalties

| Species | Betta | Guppy | Neon tetra | Panda corydoras | Otocinclus |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Betta** | Avoid | Avoid | Situational | Situational | Situational |
| **Guppy** | Avoid | Good | Good | Good | Good |
| **Neon tetra** | Situational | Good | Good | Good | Good |
| **Panda corydoras** | Situational | Good | Good | Good | Good |
| **Otocinclus** | Situational | Good | Good | Good | Good |

### Compatibility notes by species

#### Betta

- best represented as a solo centerpiece fish
- should strongly discourage housing with other bettas
- should discourage guppies because flowing fins and activity can trigger conflict
- may coexist with peaceful bottom-dwellers or calm community fish only in carefully
  authored situations

#### Guppy

- should thrive in breeder or community setups
- should be treated as poor betta tankmates in most default recommendations
- should be compatible with corydoras and Otocinclus in peaceful tropical tanks

#### Neon tetra

- should require a school to behave well
- should not be treated as a general-purpose "works anywhere" fish
- should become stressed around persistent territorial aggression

#### Panda corydoras

- should function as peaceful bottom-dwellers
- should provide mild cleanup support through detritus/scrap reduction
- should encourage use of the tank floor as a meaningful gameplay space

#### Otocinclus

- should provide algae-control support
- should not fully replace player cleaning
- should prefer stable, mature tanks rather than brand-new neglected setups

## Early progression ladder

The early game should give the player meaningful choice rather than a single forced
path.

### Guest sandbox

Guest sandbox should allow one tank slot, but the player should be able to choose a
starting template from exactly these three day-one options:

- betta display tank
- tropical community tank
- planted showcase tank

The guppy breeder setup should remain outside the guest day-one template list and
be reserved for career-oriented progression or later expansion.

Recommended guest sandbox content:

- preset backdrops
- a small but satisfying decor and plant library
- betta
- guppy
- neon tetra
- panda corydoras

Otocinclus can remain account-only or early account-unlock content if we want algae
gameplay to remain part of account progression.

### Career Tier 1: Freshwater starter

Unlocks:

- 5-gallon betta display tank
- 10-gallon community/breeder tank
- betta
- guppy
- neon tetra
- panda corydoras
- basic heater
- basic filter
- starter substrate
- starter plants
- starter preset backdrops

Player lesson:

- choose whether to make a beautiful display tank, a schooling community tank, or a
  practical breeder setup

### Career Tier 2: Tank stability tools

Unlocks:

- 15-gallon planted tank
- Otocinclus
- better plants and shelters
- algae-management guidance
- improved filter/heater variants
- more backdrop options

Player lesson:

- tank upkeep and ecosystem balance matter
- support species can improve tank performance, but they add bioload and cost

### Career Tier 3: Breeding specialization

Unlocks:

- additional breeder-friendly equipment
- guppy-focused breeding tools
- lineage and fry-management improvements
- second or third tank expansion for specialization

Player lesson:

- specialization creates more profit, but also more operational complexity

## First playable roster recommendation

If the very first implementation needs to be even smaller than the full launch
roster, start with:

1. Betta
2. Guppy
3. Neon tetra
4. Panda corydoras

Then add Otocinclus as the first post-prototype ecosystem-support species.

## Implementation guidance for future agents

- Do not treat tank archetypes as only aesthetic presets; they should influence
  stocking guidance, warnings, and progression recommendations.
- Betta display tanks should be first-class content, not an edge case.
- Cleanup species should reduce algae or waste pressure, but never eliminate
  maintenance entirely.
- The compatibility matrix should drive both placement warnings and observed fish
  behavior in the renderer/simulation.
